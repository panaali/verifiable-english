# W1 norms integration gaps

RD-8 senses documents and the integrated soft-norms batch path share `ve/document.py` without duplicating either reader: Lean supplies the projected senses environment for English fences, and the soft path batches authored norm rules. A document carrying `senses` together with `soft-norms@0`, `rights@0`, `reports@0`, `norm-defaults@0`, `readings@1`, the quantity schema, or `ve-document@2` still raises `UnsupportedProfile`. RD-8 specifies this boundary: those routes construct their own formula environments, and no common Lean admission route yet proves that the sense projection composes with their premises and identities. The ordinary senses document and ordinary soft-norm document paths remain available independently.

The merge keeps the W1 norms implementation and the integration branch's document, definition, n-ary, and data implementations. The shared declaration decoders now accept their fields together; a regulated predicate cannot also be defined or carry a functional/total flag, as the norms spec requires.

The Lean `ve_norms_route` still constructs its `Norms.Tree` with an empty definition list and uses a Background that omits W5 flags. Passing such inputs through that route would silently omit premises. The route therefore returns `UnsupportedProfile` when a norms construct is used with a defined predicate or with a functional/total data flag. W3 definition and W5 flag cases without a norms construct continue through the P0 route. The Python norms path can use definition and flag Background premises, but its norm dependency order is over regulated symbols alone and its conflict expansion does not substitute defined bodies; the mixed W1/W3 correctness and three-way parity required by `docs/spec/norms.md` sections 5–6 remain open. The Rust core validates regulated declarations, definitions, and flags, but does not implement norm document elaboration.

The FOL W2 admission path composes with active norm units. Closure or n-ary norm document admission remains explicitly deferred in both Python and Lean. These gaps need a shared dependency order over defined and regulated predicates, interleaved layer premises, flag-aware conflict bases, and corresponding Lean proofs and differential tests.

The wire-contract gate, the S0 `VE.Compose` proofs, and the S1 `readings@1` route build and audit together. The S1 executable validates reading candidates and separating situations through its own route, while the wire-contract pilot still diagnoses only its soft-norms cases and existing route inputs. It does not diagnose or certify the new readings route. Extending the contract tables and corpus to readings, and wiring the general `dispatch` into document admission beyond S1, belong to later composition stages in `docs/spec/composition.md`.

The agent-policy use case keeps its `data@0` flags and `norms@0` rule checks. Its Lean norms replay deliberately uses the flag-free environment because the norms route rejects data flags; the P0 replay checks the flagged formulas separately. The newly integrated `speech-acts@0` route admits only FOL, norms and speech acts, so the use case's confirmation facts and reporting obligations cannot yet be represented as checked speech events in the same environment as its data flags. `soft-norms@0` is also a distinct defeasible policy profile: the use case's four default variants and hard-rule verdicts have no proven translation into soft decisions. The existing harness retains its original claims and tests; a shared flagged norms/speech route and a semantics-preserving hard-to-soft translation would be needed to claim either combined result.

The agent-policy `unspecified` re-run uses `norm-defaults@0` and its dedicated W1 route. The alternatives and risk routes still consume completed deny/allow norms; they reject `norm-defaults@0` at the profile gate instead of treating a silent permission as decided. The three-valued alternatives and risk behavior specified in `docs/spec/norm-defaults.md` remains open until those routes replay the filling and prove their reports against it. This gap does not affect the separate BlueDot alternatives checks, whose environment has no unspecified default.

`speech-acts@0` uses its separate `ve_speech_acts_route` over FOL and active W1 norms. Its environment, scope legend, command and question source wrapper, and Lean audit are preserved alongside the integration branch's document and profile routes. The speech spec admits only `relational-fol@0`, `norms@0`, and `speech-acts@0`; combinations with soft norms, reports, alternatives, definitions, data, closure, order, time, or quantity remain rejected by the profile gate. These routes do not share a proved admission and background composition for command demands and their other statement layers. The stage-1 `ve_service` has no speech-acts mode, and the assurance manifest does not assign the speech route a migrated authority.

`rights@0` and `speech-acts@0` do not combine. Rights requires `soft-norms@0`, which the speech spec excludes, so the Python profile gate and both Lean routes reject an environment that lists both with `UnsupportedProfile`, and neither Lean route accepts the other's case kinds (`tests/test_rights_lean.py` pins this). Commanding or asking about a held right, or letting a right or bar license an addressee's compliance, would need a speech semantics over soft decisions, a shared admission order, and composition proofs. Neither spec defines these.

`soft-norms@0` now enters the W2 document path through the soft environment, regenerated decisions, soft premises, Lean elaboration and graph admission; its coverage report retains the open exception and weighing residuals alongside W2 status counts. The soft route still rejects W3 definitions, W5 data flags and non-FOL checker families, as its phase gate states. The shared document wrapper does not make those combinations sound: they need a common background and dependency order, a corresponding Lean decoder, and composition proofs. The service and assurance operation manifest likewise do not yet assign a migrated authority to the new soft route.

The W7 mixed English corpus now exercises the W4 n-ary atom together with the existing connectives and quantifiers. Its explicit templates pass the per-input all-derivations gate. The Lean English lexical model and Python/Lean differential still cover the v0 unary and binary core only; they do not prove one-reading or renderer correspondence for n-ary templates, data attribute wording, definition sentences, or norm blocks. Those additions have their own Python round-trip and ambiguity tests, but the W7 cross-language claim does not yet extend to them. The mixed harness also generates formulas rather than W1 norm document units, so the norm-block fixtures requested by `docs/spec/norms.md` section 2.7 remain outside that corpus.

The independent all-derivations gate now shares the ordinary English whitespace and capitalization normalization even when W1 supplies its rule-block grammar. The W1 rule-block importer still parses numbered headers and consequences with exact spacing and line boundaries before calling that gate, so editable whitespace accepted in ordinary English is not yet accepted across an entire norm document. Its identity manifest also hashes the original bytes and records original spans. Extending whitespace editing to W1 imports needs a source-offset map through the block parser and manifest checks.

The existing `ve.assist.recovery.RecoveryService` remains a single-formula recovery workflow. Its replay, review, and deadline behavior composes with the merged core for those formula actions, but it has no document-unit or norm-rule actions: W8's `add_unit`, `add_reading`, `narrow`, and `add_norm` handoff in `docs/spec/document.md` and `docs/spec/norms.md` is still open. A norm block cannot be approved through this service as a norm document, and the new document, definition, data, and n-ary surfaces do not acquire W8 authoring coverage merely by being available to the core parsers. W8 needs explicit unit identities, metadata-aware English round trips, situation checks, and replay rules before that coverage can be claimed.

The new `ve.prose@1` presentation API round-trips formula statements with paragraph and role metadata. W2 `ve-document@1` also admits source units, reading sets, mixed spans, and W1 norm blocks, which use the separate document renderer and norm English surface. There is no `ve.prose@1` round trip for that full W2 wrapper or its norm blocks yet. A client must keep the W2 metadata and norm identities on the document path rather than treating the formula presentation as a lossless export of the whole source document.
