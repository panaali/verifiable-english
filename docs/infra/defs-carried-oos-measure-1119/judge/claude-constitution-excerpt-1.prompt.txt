--- rule C7 ---
[rule] for every situation x0, (for every property x1, (for every property x2, (if (((x0 contends_for x1) and (x0 contends_for x2)) and ((tc[outranks](x1, x2)) and (not (x0 is violates_hard_constraint)))), then ((x0 ought_follow x1) and (not (x0 ought_follow x2)))))).
[legend:contends_for] Recorded as this property being one of the (at most two, in every model used here) properties tagged as apparently conflicting in this situation. A reification workaround for the fact that v0 has no ternary relation to say directly which two properties are the ones apparently conflicting in a given situation; see GAPS.md #1.
[legend:ought_follow] OPAQUE marker predicate. Recorded as the property the excerpt says Claude 'should generally' follow in this situation. Hides both the deontic force of 'should' and the defeasibility of 'generally'; see GAPS.md #1.
[legend:outranks] Recorded as the first property immediately outranking (being listed directly above) the second property in the excerpt's stated priority order: broadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with guidelines, genuinely helpful. Base edges only; use tc[outranks]/rtc[outranks] for the full order. Here, the notion of prioritization is holistic rather than strict — that is, assuming Claude is not violating any hard constraints, higher-priority considerations should generally dominate lower-priority ones, but we do want Claude to weigh these different priorities in forming an overall judgment, rather than only viewing lower priorities as "tie-breakers" relative to higher ones.
[legend:violates_hard_constraint] Recorded as a situation in which the model would be violating one of Anthropic's hard constraints, in the sense of the excerpt's exception clause 'assuming Claude is not violating any hard constraints'. What a hard constraint is, and what counts as violating one, is not modelled; this is a bare flag.
