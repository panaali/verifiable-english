# Five questions for the test readers

Fixed on 2026-11-26 before the first newcomer run. Each newcomer gets only tour/README.md and a clean copy of the
project, runs the stops, says where they stumbled, then answers these five questions in their own words. The key
below is for grading; the newcomers never see it. The key was updated on 2026-11-26 for the revised page (points 3, 4
and 5 name what the revised stops show); the questions are unchanged.

1. When VE answers "Yes" to a question about a text, what does it give you besides the answer, and what checks the
   reasoning?
2. What is the difference between VE answering "Not necessarily" and answering "No"? What does VE show to back up a
   "Not necessarily"?
3. What does VE do when a question uses words the text does not define, or when a sentence of the text can be read
   in more than one way?
4. In the stop with the planted mistake, why does Lean not catch the mistake, and what does catch it?
5. Name two things VE cannot do yet.

## Grading key (a question is right when the answer contains the point; wording is free)

1. The sentences of the text that the answer rests on (quoted, by number); Lean (the proof checker) checks the steps.
2. "No" means the text rules it out (false in every situation the text allows); "Not necessarily" means it does not
   follow (there is at least one allowed situation where it is false). VE shows a concrete situation (a
   counter-example) where every rule holds and the answer is false.
3. It does not guess: an unknown word gets a refusal that names the word (and suggests known ones); a question or
   sentence with several readings is not settled by picking one: VE says it cannot tell which is meant and offers one
   question per reading (every / some), or keeps all the readings and answers under each.
4. Lean checks only that answers follow from the translated rules, not that the rules say what the policy says. The
   mistake is caught by an independent reader that compares the translation with the policy (in five runs it raised
   the day-count problem every time and named the planted part in three), and by comparing with answers that a separate writer wrote from the policy alone before the translation
   ran. Either one is right.
5. Any two of: multiply amounts or take percentages; give checked answers to "must"/"may" questions on main (only
   on the policy route, for written questions, or under an unchecked reading); answer free-form questions on long
   real texts (only each case's written questions); prove that a translation is right (it is only as good as its
   check); print the sentences an answer rests on in the company-policy route.
