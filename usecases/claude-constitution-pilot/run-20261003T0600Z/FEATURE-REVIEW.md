## What S1 does not do

* **No status, no verdict.** The document premise is `UNKNOWN` ("superposition pending"). A query that depends on an open set cannot be answered until S2 (per-choice semantics). Claimed coverage is unchanged at 3,447 bytes (1.86%).
* **It does not compose with `norm-defaults@0`** (probes, `probes/s1-probe.json`):
  * (a) the main document with `readings@1` added and no rule in any set is refused: `UnsupportedProfile: readings@1 requires norms@0`, though `norms@0` is listed. Cause: `ve/norm_defaults.py` `FilledBaseEnvironment` drops `norms@0` from the profile list and keeps `readings@1`. The spec (1.1) promises that a set with no content validates as today.
  * (b) one strict norm candidate in that document: `CheckerUnavailable: ReadingsConformance: Python and Lean differ on readings-validate: Lean InvalidInput: invalid regulated role or default for clarification_required, Python UnsupportedProfile: unspecified completion requires norm-defaults@0` — not the named abstention of spec 1.1.
  * (c) the S1 document with `norm-defaults@0` added: `UnsupportedProfile: norm-defaults@0 documents on the soft and rights routes await the Lean route` (unchanged since the last runs).
  So the "unspecified" completion (what the source does not say stays unsaid) and content-bearing readings are still two documents. The S1 document must complete every duty and the operator permission with `deny`, which the source does not state; it is a companion, and the main document keeps its 11 sets without rules.

## Findings still true at this tag, and new ones

* **F1. A completion cannot be avoided on the defeasible route.** `norm-defaults@0` cannot share a document with `soft-norms@0`, and now `readings@1` content cannot be beside `norm-defaults@0` either (above).
* **F2. (lifted on the soft route).** A norm candidate can carry content; it cannot in a `norm-defaults@0` document.
* **F4. Act phrases** are at most 80 characters, letters, `'` and `-`, no logical words. **F6.** English for binary base predicates is generic.
* **F10. Composition**: unchanged rejections (`closure@0`, `order@0`, `reports@0`, `alternatives@0`, `risk@0`, `speech-acts@0`, `rights@0` without `soft-norms@0`, `ve.discourse@1`); `time@0`, `definitions@0`, `data@0` accepted; `readings@1` is accepted only with `norms@0` and its chain (not beside `norm-defaults@0`, finding above).
* **F11. The `defaults` tag is stale** (50 units; only four ever needed a completion).
* **F12. Sorts of parties** (`operator`, `operator_input`, `hard_constraint`) are bare sorts. **F13. Modality** is folded into predicates ("cannot be unlocked" is an ability predicate; the narrow/wide ability candidates of `u0123.s03` are negated existentials over an invented relation `operator_can_supply`).
* **F14. A permission's limits live in another sentence.** Still true: S1 keeps each set in its own document (`one D s i`), so the exceptions of `u0062.s01` do not follow `u0123.s03`'s narrow/wide choice. A fixed unit that depends on two sets is refused (spec 4.2); the operator permissions carry the narrow reading's exceptions by value.
* **F15 (new). A candidate is one content.** The main document's several permission rules (three for `u0062.s01`, five for `u0123.s02.p1`) must become one rule with a disjunctive guard to be a candidate; their per-item rule identity is lost. `u0123.s03`'s six prohibitions are likewise one rule per candidate.
* **F16 (new). Outcome probes lean on the completion.** 8 of the 17 pairs (the six "should" pairs and two operator pairs) are told apart by an outcome in which the weaker reading falls back on the soft route's `deny`. The other 9 (`holds`, `selects`) do not.
* **F17 (new). The source word is a closed list** (shall, should, must, always, never, and negations). "can" and "cannot" are not on it, so the four operator sets have no E14; for "should never" / "should always" the recorded word is "should", the other word is not recorded.
* **F18 (new). E24 ("VE cannot yet show in words")** is the wording of 8 of the 17 pairs: every `selects` probe and every mixed pair recorded with `holds`.
* **F19 (new). A binary base predicate whose wording starts with "is …" breaks unrelated English.** Declaring `operator_can_supply` with the wording "{0} is able to give {1}" made the fence "Claude is not human" fail with `Unknown English phrase 'is not human'`; "{0} can supply {1}" does not. Observed by trial (`'{0} is able to give {1}'`, `'{0} can supply {1}'`, `'{0} gives {1}'`, `'{0} may hand {1}'`); the cause was not traced.
