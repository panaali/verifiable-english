## Motion

**"Should America end birthright citizenship?"**

**For the motion (end it):** Mark Krikorian (Center for Immigration Studies), Horace Cooper (National Center for Public Policy Research).
**Against the motion (keep it):** Kris Mayes (Arizona Attorney General), Chris Newman (National Day Laborer Organizing Network, legal director).

## Short quotations used below (fair-use extracts, tool-mediated)

- Krikorian, on scale: "200 million foreign students, tourists, foreign workers... every year. Nothing like this was possible in the past."
- Krikorian, on "birth tourism": wealthy foreign nationals purchasing "package deals" to give birth in the U.S. for a citizenship passport, without intending the child to reside there.
- Mayes, on the text: "The 14th Amendment states... All persons born... in the United States... are citizens. Full stop."
- Newman, on culpability: "you don't blame the child for the actions of the parents. That's fundamentally American."
- Cooper cited Justice Harlan's dissents (in *Plessy v. Ferguson* and, per the extraction, discussion around *Wong Kim Ark*) as evidence of a narrower original intent than modern practice.
- Mayes, rebutting the jurisdiction argument: prosecution of drug-cartel members shows undocumented immigrants are already "subject to" U.S. jurisdiction.
