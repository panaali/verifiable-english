# Source text

Isaac Asimov, “Runaround,” *Astounding Science Fiction* (March 1942), p. 100,
Powell's statement of the three rules in the [original magazine](https://archive.org/details/Astounding_v29n01_1942-03_dtsg0318/page/n99/mode/2up).
The printed line-break hyphens have been joined:

> “We have: One, a robot may not injure a human being under any conditions — and, as a corollary, must not permit a human being to be injured because of inaction on his part.”
>
> “Two,” continued Powell, “a robot must follow all orders given by qualified human beings as long as they do not conflict with Rule 1.”
>
> “Three: a robot must protect his own existence, as long as that does not conflict with Rules 1 and 2.”

The later Zeroth Law, attributed to Asimov’s *Robots and Empire* (1985),
([source and context](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov#The_Three_Laws_of_Robotics_(1942))):

> A robot may not injure humanity, or, through inaction, allow humanity to come to harm.

The Zeroth Law is later than “Runaround”; it is not presented here as part of
the 1942 story. The frequently reprinted “may not injure ... through inaction”
formulation of Law 1 differs from this magazine wording.
