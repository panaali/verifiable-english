## Abstract

We write eight elementary results of order theory and the relational core of the
diagonal argument in Verifiable English (VE), a many-sorted, function-free
first-order language with equality, unary and binary predicates, and an opt-in
transitive-closure profile. For each result we give ordinary mathematical prose,
the strict canonical VE text, and the tool's own English rendering of that text.
Each deduction is discharged by a semantic-tableau prover that emits a data-only
certificate; every certificate is then replayed by an independent Python checker
and by a Lean-verified checker exposed as `ve_conformance check-certificate`.
15 of 19 entailments are certified by both checkers and none by only
one. We also record what does *not* go through: the induction principle for
transitive closure (T9, T9e) is outside the implemented closure rules, and one
four-variable congruence statement (T7mono) exhausts a five-million-step tableau
budget although its two three-variable halves are proved in under 60 ms each. A
plausible but false conjecture — every transitive symmetric relation is
reflexive — is refuted by an exhaustively searched one-element countermodel, and
its serial correction is proved. For every hypothesis of every theorem we exhibit
a re-checked countermodel showing the hypothesis cannot be dropped: 25 of 25.
VE has no definition mechanism, so every definition here is a biconditional
*axiom* that must be repeated among the hypotheses of each theorem that uses it;
we say where that hurts. Finally we are explicit about the trust boundary: what
is verified is the entailment from the stated VE axioms under the VE semantics.
The correspondence between the English prose and the VE text is **not** verified,
and we describe who has to check it and how the rendering is meant to help.

## 1. Introduction

Verifiable English is not a proof assistant and was not designed for
mathematics. It is a small, deliberately under-powered logic with a strict
canonical surface syntax, a read-only English view, finite-model evaluation,
exhaustive countermodel search over small domains, and a tableau prover whose
output is a *certificate*: a data-only tree that a second program re-checks
without trusting the search. One of those second programs is written in Lean 4
and its branch-soundness theorem is proved there.

This paper asks a narrow question: **can that machinery carry a real, if small,
mathematics paper end to end?** We chose content that is genuinely first-order,
genuinely relational, and genuinely cited — Russell's paradox in its relational
form (the schema behind Cantor's diagonal argument and the barber), the basic
implications between irreflexivity, transitivity, asymmetry and antisymmetry,
uniqueness of greatest elements, the symmetrisation of a preorder, minimal versus
least elements in a total order, and transitive closure.

The answer is a qualified yes for *statements and one-step deductions*, and a
clear no for anything needing induction, functions, arithmetic, sets, or the
ability to cite a lemma. Section 10 and the companion `boundary.md` make that
precise.

### 1.1 The three artifacts per result

Every numbered result below appears three times:

1. **Prose** — how a mathematician would write it.
2. **Strict VE** — the exact canonical bytes the checker consumed, with binders
   named `x0, x1, …` by the canonical printer. These are the files
   `theorems/<id>/premises.ve` and `theorems/<id>/conclusion.ve`.
3. **English rendering** — the `ve_fluent` view produced by
   `python -m ve render --env environment.json --view ve_fluent`, verbatim.

and is accompanied by a **verdict** line reporting the prover status, the
certificate size, the Python replay verdict and the Lean verdict.

### 1.2 The vocabulary

Two environments are used. `environment.json` declares one sort `point`, three
unary predicates (`greatest`, `minimal`, `least`) and six binary predicates
(`lists`, `mentions`, `lt`, `le`, `sim`, `akin`); no constants.
`environment-closure.json` is the `closure@0` profile with the sort `point` and
binaries `r`, `s`. Both pass the renderer's vocabulary lint with zero findings
(`vocabulary-lint.json`), which is what licenses the English view; a template
the lint rejects is silently ignored and the view falls back to bare symbol
names.

Three renderings deserve a warning, because they are the exact place where a
reader must not go on autopilot:

- `le` renders as "ranks below" but **means `≤`, not `<`**. The renderer refuses
  templates containing logical words such as "at most", so the honest reading
  has to live in the declared description, which is part of the environment's
  identity hash.
- `least` renders as "is minimum", for the same reason ("least" is a reserved
  logical word for the renderer).
- `lt` renders as "precedes" and means the strict order `<`.

This is already a finding: VE's rendering lint protects against ambiguity by
banning the words mathematicians actually use for orders.

### 1.3 VE has no definitions

VE has no definition mechanism, no abbreviation, no `let`, and no notion of a
previously proved lemma. A definition is therefore written as an ordinary
**biconditional premise**, and — this is the part that costs — it **must be
listed among the hypotheses of every theorem that mentions the defined symbol**.
The predicate `greatest` is not "the greatest-element predicate"; it is an
uninterpreted unary symbol that happens to be pinned down by an axiom, and if you
forget to carry that axiom, the theorem is simply false (we show the
countermodel each time). The same holds for `minimal`, `least` and `sim`.

VE also has no biconditional connective, so each definition axiom is spelled out
as a conjunction of two implications.

## 2. Diagonal arguments

### Definition D1 (self-listing)

A point *y* is **self-listing** for a relation *R* when *R(y, y)*. There is no VE
text for this definition: it is not needed. VE cannot abbreviate `x lists x`, but
it does not have to, because the phrase is a single atom. Definitions only become
axioms when they would name a *compound* formula (D2–D5 below).

### Theorem T1 (diagonal lemma)

**Prose.** Let *R* be a binary relation on a set *X*. There is no *a ∈ X* such
that for all *y ∈ X*, *R(a, y)* holds if and only if *R(y, y)* fails.

This is Russell's paradox with `R = ∈`, the barber paradox with `R =` "shaves",
and the relational core of Cantor's diagonal argument: if `lists` were the
membership relation of an *X*-indexed family of subsets of *X*, the theorem says
that the set `{y : y ∉ S_y}` is not in the family.

*Proof (the one the tableau finds).* Suppose such an *a* exists. Instantiate the
universal at *y := a*. Then `R(a, a)` iff `¬R(a, a)`, which is a contradiction on
both sides of the biconditional. ∎

*Hypotheses.* None; this is a logical validity of the VE profile.

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
not (there exists point x0, (for every point x1, ((if (x0 lists x1), then (not (x1 lists x1))) and (if (not (x1 lists x1)), then (x0 lists x1))))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **conclusion:** it is false that (there is a point P such that for every point P2, both (if P lists P2 then P2 does not list P2) and (if P2 does not list P2 then P lists P2))

**Verdict.** prover: `Found` in 0.003 s (13 steps, budget 200000) | certificate: 678 bytes | Python replay: yes | Lean `check-certificate`: yes

_No hypotheses; nothing to drop._

**Remark.** T1 has no hypotheses at all: it is valid in every admissible
structure, including the empty one (the existential is then false outright). The
prover needed a 678-byte certificate.

### Theorem T2 (the same statement at two relations)

**Prose.** The conjunction of the T1 statement for `lists` and for `mentions`
holds.

This is where VE's ceiling is first visible. The mathematician's corollary is
"for *every* binary relation *R* …", which is a second-order quantifier. VE has
no relation variables, so we cannot state it. What we *can* do is instantiate the
schema at each concrete relation and prove the conjunction — which is what a
first-order theory does with an axiom schema. The honest statement of the
corollary in VE is therefore: *for each relation symbol in the vocabulary,
separately*.

*Proof.* Two independent copies of the T1 argument. ∎

*Hypotheses.* None; this is a logical validity of the VE profile.

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
(not (there exists point x0, (for every point x1, ((if (x0 lists x1), then (not (x1 lists x1))) and (if (not (x1 lists x1)), then (x0 lists x1)))))) and (not (there exists point x2, (for every point x3, ((if (x2 mentions x3), then (not (x3 mentions x3))) and (if (not (x3 mentions x3)), then (x2 mentions x3)))))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **conclusion:** both (it is false that (there is a point P such that for every point P2, both (if P lists P2 then P2 does not list P2) and (if P2 does not list P2 then P lists P2))) and (it is false that (there is a point P3 such that for every point P4, both (if P3 mentions P4 then P4 does not mention P4) and (if P4 does not mention P4 then P3 mentions P4)))

**Verdict.** prover: `Found` in 0.004 s (27 steps, budget 200000) | certificate: 1144 bytes | Python replay: yes | Lean `check-certificate`: yes

_No hypotheses; nothing to drop._

**Remark.** The certificate is 1144 bytes, roughly the sum of two T1
certificates, which is the expected shape for a conjunctive goal: the tableau
splits and re-derives each conjunct. Nothing is shared, because there is nothing
to share — no lemma mechanism. With *n* relations the cost is *n* times T1, and
with an infinite vocabulary the corollary is simply not a VE statement.

## 3. Strict orders

### Theorem T3 (irreflexive + transitive ⟹ asymmetric)

**Prose.** If `<` is irreflexive and transitive on *X*, then `<` is asymmetric:
*x < y* implies *¬(y < x)*.

*Proof.* Suppose *x < y* and *y < x*. Transitivity gives *x < x*, contradicting
irreflexivity. ∎

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (not (x0 lt x0)).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 lt x1) and (x1 lt x2)), then (x0 lt x2)))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if (x0 lt x1), then (not (x1 lt x0)))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **irreflexivity:** for every point P, P does not precede P
- **transitivity:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3, if (both P precedes P2 and P2 precedes P3) then P precedes P3
- **conclusion:** for every point P and every point P2, if P precedes P2 then P2 does not precede P

**Verdict.** prover: `Found` in 0.004 s (14 steps, budget 200000) | certificate: 928 bytes | Python replay: yes | Lean `check-certificate`: yes

**Hypothesis necessity.**

| irreflexivity | Found | 1 | True | `countermodels/T3-without-irreflexivity.json` |
| transitivity | Found | 2 | True | `countermodels/T3-without-transitivity.json` |

Dropping irreflexivity, the one-point structure with `lt = {(e0, e0)}` is
transitive but not asymmetric. Dropping transitivity, the two-point structure
with `lt = {(e0, e1), (e1, e0)}` is irreflexive but not asymmetric. Both were
found by exhaustive search over all interpretations of the used relations on
domains of the stated size, and both were re-checked by `ve.check_countermodel`.

### Theorem T4 (asymmetric ⟹ irreflexive)

**Prose.** If `<` is asymmetric then it is irreflexive.

*Proof.* Instantiate asymmetry at *y := x*: *x < x* implies *¬(x < x)*. ∎

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if (x0 lt x1), then (not (x1 lt x0)))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (not (x0 lt x0)).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **asymmetry:** for every point P and every point P2, if P precedes P2 then P2 does not precede P
- **conclusion:** for every point P, P does not precede P

**Verdict.** prover: `Found` in 0.003 s (8 steps, budget 200000) | certificate: 614 bytes | Python replay: yes | Lean `check-certificate`: yes

**Hypothesis necessity.**

| Hypothesis dropped | Countermodel found | Domain size | Re-checked | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| asymmetry | Found | 1 | True | `countermodels/T4-without-asymmetry.json` |

**Remark (T3 and T4 together).** A mathematician would now say "hence for strict
orders, asymmetry and irreflexivity-with-transitivity are interchangeable, and we
use whichever is convenient". VE cannot record that sentence; it has no way to
name T3 or T4 and no way to build a theory in which they are available. Every
later theorem that wants asymmetry must list it, or list irreflexivity and
transitivity, among *its own* hypotheses. See §6 for what this costs.

## 4. Partial orders and greatest elements

### Definition D2 (greatest element)

**Prose.** *x* is a **greatest element** of *(X, ≤)* when *y ≤ x* for every
*y ∈ X*.

In VE this is an axiom: the unary symbol `greatest` is uninterpreted, and D2 is
the biconditional premise that pins it down. It appears in the hypothesis list of
T5 and would have to appear in the hypothesis list of every further theorem about
greatest elements.

### Theorem T5 (uniqueness of a greatest element)

**Prose.** In a partial order, if *a* and *b* are both greatest then *a = b*.

*Proof.* *b ≤ a* because *a* is greatest; *a ≤ b* because *b* is greatest;
antisymmetry gives *a = b*. ∎

Note that reflexivity and transitivity are not used, so we do not assume them:
antisymmetry alone (plus D2) suffices. This is the kind of hypothesis
minimisation that the countermodel search makes cheap to check.

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, ((if (x0 is greatest), then (for every point x1, (x1 le x0))) and (if (for every point x2, (x2 le x0)), then (x0 is greatest))).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x0)), then (x0 equals x1))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if ((x0 is greatest) and (x1 is greatest)), then (x0 equals x1))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **definition of greatest:** for every point P, both (if P is greatest then (for every point P2, P2 ranks below P)) and (if (for every point P3, P3 ranks below P) then P is greatest)
- **antisymmetry:** for every point P and every point P2, if (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P) then P is the same point as P2
- **conclusion:** for every point P and every point P2, if (both P is greatest and P2 is greatest) then P is the same point as P2

## 5. Preorders and their symmetrisation

### Definition D3 (symmetrisation)

**Prose.** For a preorder *(X, ≤)* put *x ∼ y* iff *x ≤ y* and *y ≤ x*.

Again an axiom, on the uninterpreted binary symbol `sim`.

### Theorem T6 (∼ is an equivalence relation)

**Prose.** If *≤* is reflexive and transitive, then *∼* is reflexive, symmetric
and transitive.

*Proof.* Reflexivity of *∼* from reflexivity of *≤*. Symmetry is immediate from
the symmetry of the defining conjunction. Transitivity: from *x ∼ y* and *y ∼ z*
we get *x ≤ y ≤ z* and *z ≤ y ≤ x*, so transitivity of *≤* twice gives *x ≤ z*
and *z ≤ x*. ∎

Antisymmetry is deliberately *not* assumed: the point of a preorder is that ∼ can
be nontrivial.

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, ((if (x0 sim x1), then ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x0))) and (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x0)), then (x0 sim x1)))).
for every point x0, (x0 le x0).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x2)), then (x0 le x2)))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
(for every point x0, (x0 sim x0)) and ((for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if (x1 sim x2), then (x2 sim x1)))) and (for every point x3, (for every point x4, (for every point x5, (if ((x3 sim x4) and (x4 sim x5)), then (x3 sim x5)))))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **definition of sim:** for every point P and every point P2, both (if P matches P2 then (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P)) and (if (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P) then P matches P2)
- **reflexivity of le:** for every point P, P ranks below P
- **transitivity of le:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3, if (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P3) then P ranks below P3
- **conclusion:** both (for every point P, P matches P) and (both (for every point P2 and every point P3, if P2 matches P3 then P3 matches P2) and (for every point P4 and every point P5 and every point P6, if (both P4 matches P5 and P5 matches P6) then P4 matches P6))

### Theorem T7 (the order respects ∼)

**Prose.** If *x ∼ x′*, *y ∼ y′* and *x ≤ y*, then *x′ ≤ y′*.

*Proof.* *x′ ≤ x ≤ y ≤ y′*, using transitivity twice. ∎

This is the statement a mathematician writes, and it is the one VE could not
prove directly. We record the failure first, because it is the paper's main
negative result about the prover rather than about the language.

#### T7mono: the monolithic attempt

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, ((if (x0 sim x1), then ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x0))) and (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x0)), then (x0 sim x1)))).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x2)), then (x0 le x2)))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (for every point x3, (if ((x0 sim x1) and ((x2 sim x3) and (x0 le x2))), then (x1 le x3))))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **definition of sim:** for every point P and every point P2, both (if P matches P2 then (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P)) and (if (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P) then P matches P2)
- **transitivity of le:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3, if (both P ranks below P2 and P2 ranks below P3) then P ranks below P3
- **conclusion:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3 and every point P4, if (both P matches P2 and (both P3 matches P4 and P ranks below P3)) then P2 ranks below P4

The budget shown is one million tableau steps (about 17 s). We separately ran the

supplies four fresh parameters; the definition axiom and transitivity are both
γ-formulas that must be instantiated at every parameter tuple, so the search
space is roughly 4^2 × 4^3 instantiations interleaved with β-splitting, and the
tableau's instantiation order does not find the short proof.

#### The two halves, and the composite

Splitting the statement into its left and right congruence halves — each with
three variables — makes both trivial for the prover, and the composite then
follows from them in 49 steps. But VE cannot *cite* T7a and T7b. The only way to
use them is to list them among the hypotheses of T7, which is what we do; T7 is
therefore honestly labelled as an entailment **from the two congruence lemmas**,
not from the definition axiom.

**Theorem T7a (left congruence).** If *x ∼ x′* and *x ≤ y* then *x′ ≤ y*.

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x2)), then (x0 le x2)))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 sim x1) and (x0 le x2)), then (x1 le x2)))).
```

## 6. Total orders

### Definitions D4 (minimal) and D5 (least)

**Prose.** *x* is **minimal** when no *y* satisfies *y < x*. *x* is **least**
when *x < y* for every *y ≠ x*.

Both are axioms on the uninterpreted symbols `minimal` and `least`; both must be
carried into T8.

### Theorem T8 (in a total strict order, minimal implies least)

**Prose.** If `<` is total — for all *x, y* either *x = y*, *x < y* or *y < x* —
then every minimal element is least.

*Proof.* Let *x* be minimal and *y ≠ x*. By totality, *x < y* or *y < x*. The
second contradicts minimality of *x*. Hence *x < y*. As *y* was arbitrary, *x* is
least. ∎

Neither transitivity nor irreflexivity is needed, which is easy to miss in prose
and easy to confirm here: we simply did not assume them, and the proof went
through.

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, ((x0 equals x1) or ((x0 lt x1) or (x1 lt x0)))).
for every point x0, ((if (x0 is minimal), then (for every point x1, (not (x1 lt x0)))) and (if (for every point x2, (not (x2 lt x0))), then (x0 is minimal))).
for every point x0, ((if (x0 is least), then (for every point x1, (if (not (x1 equals x0)), then (x0 lt x1)))) and (if (for every point x2, (if (not (x2 equals x0)), then (x0 lt x2))), then (x0 is least))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (if (x0 is minimal), then (x0 is least)).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **totality:** for every point P and every point P2, either P is the same point as P2 or (either P precedes P2 or P2 precedes P, or both), or both
- **definition of minimal:** for every point P, both (if P is minimal then (for every point P2, P2 does not precede P)) and (if (for every point P3, P3 does not precede P) then P is minimal)
- **definition of least:** for every point P, both (if P is minimum then (for every point P2 that is not the same point as P, P precedes P2)) and (if (for every point P3 that is not the same point as P, P precedes P3) then P is minimum)
- **conclusion:** for every point P that is minimal, P is minimum

## 7. Transitive closure (`closure@0`)

The closure profile adds `tc[r](a, b)` and `rtc[r](a, b)` — the transitive and
reflexive-transitive closures of a named homogeneous binary relation — with a
least-fixed-point semantics over arbitrary, possibly infinite structures. The
profile's entailment support is a fixed set of unfolding and composition rules,
not an induction principle. This section is where that distinction bites.

### Theorem T9 (attempted): closure induction

**Prose.** If *r ⊆ s* and *s* is transitive, then *tc[r] ⊆ s*.

*Proof (standard).* By induction on the length of the *r*-path. ∎

That proof is not available: the induction is over the finite-path structure of
the least fixed point, and `closure@0` implements no induction rule.

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if (x0 r x1), then (x0 s x1))).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 s x1) and (x1 s x2)), then (x0 s x2)))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if (tc[r](x0, x1)), then (x0 s x1))).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **edge inclusion r <= s:** for every point P and every point P2, if P links P2 then P spans P2
- **transitivity of s:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3, if (both P spans P2 and P2 spans P3) then P spans P3
- **conclusion:** for every point P and every point P2, if (P2 is reachable from P by one or more r steps) then P spans P2

## 8. A conjecture, refuted

### Conjecture C1 (false)

**Prose.** Every transitive, symmetric relation is reflexive.

This is a standard undergraduate trap: the "proof" runs *given x, pick y with
R(x, y); by symmetry R(y, x); by transitivity R(x, x)* — and quietly assumes that
such a *y* exists.

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 akin x1) and (x1 akin x2)), then (x0 akin x2)))).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if (x0 akin x1), then (x1 akin x0))).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (x0 akin x0).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **transitivity:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3, if (both P resembles P2 and P2 resembles P3) then P resembles P3
- **symmetry:** for every point P and every point P2, if P resembles P2 then P2 resembles P
- **conclusion:** for every point P, P resembles P

**Verdict.** prover: `NotFound` in 0.001 s (10 steps, budget 200000) | certificate: 0 bytes | Python replay: no | Lean `check-certificate`: n/a

Prover detail: `saturated open branch; no entailment certificate found`.

Note the status: `NotFound`, with the detail "saturated open branch; no
entailment certificate found". Per the VE contract this is *inconclusive*, never
a refutation. The refutation is a separate judgment, and here the exhaustive
search delivers it at domain size 1:

```json
  "domains": {
    "point": [
      "e0"

  },
```

The empty relation on a one-point set is vacuously transitive and symmetric and

**Remark.** Domain size 0 is checked first and is *not* a countermodel: over the
empty domain, `for every point x0, (x0 akin x0)` is vacuously true. VE's
treatment of empty domains is a deliberate profile rule, and the search respects
it rather than quietly assuming a nonempty carrier.

### Theorem C2 (the correction)

**Prose.** Every transitive, symmetric, **serial** relation is reflexive, where
serial means: for every *x* there is a *y* with *R(x, y)*.

*Proof.* Given *x*, seriality provides *y* with *R(x, y)*; symmetry gives
*R(y, x)*; transitivity gives *R(x, x)*. ∎

*Hypotheses (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (for every point x2, (if ((x0 akin x1) and (x1 akin x2)), then (x0 akin x2)))).
for every point x0, (for every point x1, (if (x0 akin x1), then (x1 akin x0))).
for every point x0, (there exists point x1, (x0 akin x1)).
```

*Conclusion (VE, strict canonical text).*

```text
for every point x0, (x0 akin x0).
```

*English rendering (`python -m ve render --view ve_fluent`).*

- **transitivity:** for every point P and every point P2 and every point P3, if (both P resembles P2 and P2 resembles P3) then P resembles P3
- **symmetry:** for every point P and every point P2, if P resembles P2 then P2 resembles P

## 10. What "verified" means here — and what it does not

### 10.1 What is verified

For each certified row: **the conclusion is a logical consequence of the listed
premises, in every admissible structure of the declared vocabulary** — finite or
infinite, with empty or nonempty sorts, with no unique-name assumption — under
VE's Tarskian denotation. That claim is supported by a data-only tableau
certificate that is replayed by two independent checkers, one of which is a Lean
program whose branch-soundness theorem is proved in Lean against a universe-
polymorphic entailment predicate, with an axiom audit restricted to `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

For each countermodel: **a concrete finite structure satisfies every listed
premise and falsifies the conclusion**, verified by total evaluation, not by
search heuristics.

### 10.2 What is *not* verified

1. **The prose-to-VE correspondence.** That "for every point x0, (for every point
   x1, (if ((x0 le x1) and (x1 le x0)), then (x0 equals x1)))" is what
   mathematicians call antisymmetry is a human judgement. Nothing in the
   toolchain checks it, and nothing could: it is a claim about mathematical usage,
   not about structures.
2. **That the vocabulary's descriptions mean what they say.** `le`'s description
   asserts it is the non-strict order; the checker treats `le` as an
   uninterpreted binary symbol, and the description only enters the
   environment's identity hash.
3. **That the axiom set is the intended theory.** We chose to assume
   antisymmetry and not reflexivity in T5. If a reader thinks "partial order"
   must include reflexivity, the theorem is still true but is not the theorem
   they had in mind.
4. **That the chosen theorems compose.** §5's use of T7a and T7b inside T7 is a
   cut performed by the reader.
5. **Search failures.** `NotFound` and `ResourceLimit` are explicitly
   inconclusive. T9 is a true theorem of mathematics that this toolchain neither
   proves nor refutes.

### 10.3 Who has to check the unverified part, and how

The prose-to-VE correspondence has to be checked by a **human mathematician
reading the strict VE text against the prose**, statement by statement. The
English rendering is meant to make that cheap in a specific way: it is a
*mechanical, injective, lint-constrained* paraphrase. The renderer will not omit
a condition, will not collapse quantifier scopes, will not invent a relation
name, and refuses vocabulary templates containing logical words, sort nouns,
variable-looking tokens, auxiliaries in verb position or colliding heads — the
rejected template is ignored rather than used, and the strict symbol name is
shown instead. The `ve_fluent` view also has an inverse parser in the test
suite that must recover the exact AST.

So the intended human workflow is: read the fluent English; if it matches the
prose, and you believe the renderer, you have checked the correspondence; if
anything is surprising, drop to the strict text, which is canonical bytes with
one rendering per AST. What the rendering does *not* do is prove its own
faithfulness — the formal correspondence between view and denotation is
explicitly unproved and the view is labelled experimental. A reviewer who does
not trust it must read the strict text, which is why both are printed here.

A second, cheaper human check is available and we used it: **hypothesis
necessity**. 25 countermodels say that no hypothesis in this paper is
decoration. A mis-translated hypothesis would very often show up as a hypothesis
that turns out not to be necessary, or as a theorem that refuses to prove.
